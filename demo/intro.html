Good tools already exist.

Each one answers a single question.

Safe BrowsingWarns about dangerous websites.Not who made a post.
TinEyeFinds matching images.Not who owns them, or if reuse was allowed.
HiveScores images for AI generation.A score isn't proof of origin.
C2PA Content CredentialsSigned creation history.Lost when a photo is screenshotted.
W3C Verifiable CredentialsA standard for proving identity.Doesn't look at content at all.
Perspective APIScores comments for toxicity.One comment at a time, with no context.

Six tools. Six websites. And you still have to connect the pieces yourself.

If nothing changes

  • Seeing stops being believing.Any video can be faked, so real evidence gets dismissed as fake too.
  • Creators stop sharing.Their work is copied, cropped and re-posted with no credit and no way to prove it was theirs.
  • Anyone can speak for an institution.Fake accounts and AI agents use names they have no right to.
  • Moderation gets it wrong both ways.Jokes and slang get punished, while real threats in context slip through.
  • Trust in everything online wears away.

ARGUS

One trust layer for everything you see and post online.

Named after the hundred-eyed watchman of Greek myth. A Chrome extension today: it works on any site, so no platform has to adopt it first.

Five engines, working together

01Content SafetyChecks what you write in any text box, in the context of the post, and offers a kinder way to say it before it's sent.
02Provenance & OwnershipGives creators a Content ID and a signed record, and recognises copies, crops and screenshots of their work.
03Authenticity & AI MediaReads Content Credentials and checks images and video for signs of AI, harm and private details.
04Identity & AccountabilityVerifiable credentials for people, organisations and AI agents, and a warning on look-alike accounts.
05Responsible AIEvery decision explained, uncertain ones sent to a person, and each post summarised once and reused.

"That's sick!" under a trick shot is praise. Under a crash report it isn't. ARGUS knows the difference.
